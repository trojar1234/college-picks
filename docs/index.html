Projections

How to read this page
  • Prediction is the model's projected score (away–home, rounded so it matches the model's spread and total) and the favorite's chance to win. Tap any game for the full breakdown: score distribution, likely range, inputs, every sportsbook's line, and weather.
  • Line is the middle of the sportsbooks' lines; Model is the model's own number. The top number is the spread, the bottom the total. Edge is the gap between the two, in points.
  • Model lean is the model's side in every game. A lean is not a bet: most are close calls (leans with an edge under 2 points are shown in grey, 2+ in bold). Only games marked Bet qualify.
  • Bet = the model and line are 7+ points apart and nothing makes the projection shaky. In past seasons these went about 60% against the spread and 64% on totals against opening lines. A bet keeps the line it was first flagged at.
  • Flag = 7+ points apart, but with a reason for caution: a QB change, a manual adjustment, wind or rain (the model doesn't use weather), an FCS team, or a gap of 10+ points. A 10+ point gap usually means news the model can't see (an injury, a suspension, a QB change). In past seasons those gaps won about as often as other flags against opening lines but only about half the time against closing lines, so they're held for a news check instead of bet automatically (the Performance page tracks how each caution does). 8–10 point bets, and lines quoted by fewer than 3 books, get a reminder to check.
  • Line moved / Model moved = a recorded bet whose edge today is under 7 points, because the line moved or the model's number changed (QB news, a correction). It's still graded at the original line, but it isn't a bet at today's number.
  • A bet needs a live sportsbook quote from the last 36 hours. When a game only has the backup line from CollegeFootballData, a 7-point gap shows as a flag instead.
  • EV is the expected profit per unit at −110 for the model's side, using probabilities calibrated on every past game. It's deliberately conservative, so small positive numbers are normal; the best-book suggestion is chosen by the same measure (line and price together). Each game's details also show the model's raw chance next to the calibrated one, and the worst price at which the bet still breaks even.
  • QB change alerts are inferred from who started each team's last game (the box score), not from a confirmed depth chart, so treat them as unverified until the news confirms the starter.
  • The model's edge is against early-week lines (about 50% against closing lines), so bets are worth acting on early. Each bet shows the best available book for that side; results are still graded at the middle of the books, which is the conservative choice. Sportsbook lines are never model inputs.
