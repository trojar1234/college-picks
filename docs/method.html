How the model works

RatingsPreseason priorsProjectionsProbabilitiesBet rulesTestingWhat it can't seeTerms

This is a market-blind college football model. It rates every team from game results and play-by-play efficiency, projects every FBS game, and compares its numbers with the sportsbooks. Sportsbook lines are never inputs: they're used only to find disagreements, grade bets and show prices.

Team ratings

Each team gets an offense and a defense rating on four measures, adjusted for every opponent it has played:

  • Efficiency: expected points added per play (EPA), with garbage time removed.
  • Success rate: how often a play gains enough to stay on schedule.
  • Pace: plays per game.
  • Scoring: points scored, with blowouts capped at a 50-point margin. This catches what per-play numbers miss: special teams, red-zone finishing, field position and turnovers.

The ratings are re-solved after every game. Early in the season they lean on the preseason prior; each game counts as one game of evidence against a prior worth 3 games, so by midseason the results dominate. Home field is estimated from the current season's games. FCS teams are rated from all their games, including FCS-only games, and shrunk toward the FCS average.

Preseason priors

Before a team plays, its rating comes from a regression trained on past seasons: last season's ratings, its average over the previous four seasons, returning production on offense and defense (counting incoming transfers), the expected starting quarterback's career efficiency, and the roster talent composite.

From ratings to a projection

The two teams' ratings give each side's expected efficiency, success rate, pace and scoring for this matchup and venue. A conversion learned from past seasons, with recent seasons weighted more, turns those into expected points for each team. The projected spread and total are those expected points.

One correction is added: Army, Navy and Air Force run option offenses that the ratings underrate, so their projections get a correction learned from earlier seasons' misses.

Probabilities

Win, cover and over chances come from a normal curve around the projection, sized by how far off past out-of-sample projections have been (about 16 points on the margin and 16 on the total). Against the sportsbooks the model's raw confidence is too high, so cover chances keep only the share that held up in the backtest: about a quarter. Spread pushes use how often games really end on each margin: about 2.6 times the smooth curve on 3 and 2.3 times on 7, and never on 0. The same function prices every line, so the published chance, the expected value and the best book always agree. A score simulation supplies the most likely final scores.

Bet rules

  • A bet is a gap of 7 or more points between the model and the middle of the sportsbooks' lines, on the spread or the total, with no caution.
  • A flag is the same gap with a caution: a quarterback change, a manual adjustment, an FCS team, wind or rain, or a gap of 10 or more points (usually news the model can't see).
  • A bet needs a live sportsbook quote from the last 36 hours, and keeps the line it was first recorded at. It's graded there at −110 (1 unit), the conservative choice, even though the site points to the best price available.
  • The edge is against early lines. Lines the model disagrees with tend to move toward it before kickoff, so bets are worth placing soon after they appear.

How it's tested

Walk-forward backtest. Every past week is predicted using only what was known before it: ratings from earlier games and a points conversion refit each week on earlier games, the same clock the live model runs on. A self-test rewrites one week's results and checks that no earlier projection moves. Those predictions are graded against the lines of the time.

Keeping or dropping an input. A new input is kept only if all three hold: the accuracy gain is real (its 95% range, resampling whole weeks, stays above zero), it doesn't cost bets against opening lines, and it doesn't make the newest complete season clearly worse. More than 40 ideas have been tested; the Performance page lists them and why they were dropped.

Live record. Every projection is frozen at kickoff and graded when the game is final. Every bet and blocked flag is written once to a decision log that is never edited.

What it can't see

  • Injuries, suspensions and depth-chart news. A quarterback change is detected from the previous game's box score and blocks the bet; anything else only shows up as a large gap with the line.
  • Weather. Wind and rain forecasts are shown and block total bets, but they aren't inputs.
  • Motivation, coaching changes during the season, and opt-outs before bowl games.

Terms

Spread, ATS
The points the favorite gives. "Against the spread" means the bet is graded after adding the spread.
Spread signs
A minus sign marks the favorite: "Texas −7" means Texas must win by more than 7; "Florida +7" means Florida covers by losing by fewer than 7 (or winning). Stored lines are from the home team's view, so −7 means the home team is favored by 7.
Model lean, bet, flag
A lean is the model's side in every game. A bet is a lean with a 7+ point edge, no caution and a fresh sportsbook quote; it's recorded and graded. A flag has the edge but is held back by a caution. A recorded bet whose edge later drops under 7 shows as "Line moved" or "Model moved".
Calibrated chance
The model's own chance scaled back toward 50% by how much of its confidence held up against past lines. "Model alone" is the chance before that.
Breaks even at
The worst price at which a bet with that calibrated chance still has positive expected value.
Total, O/U
The combined points; a bet on the over or the under.
Edge
Points between the model's number and the line.
Opening and closing lines
The first and last lines before kickoff. Closing lines are the sharpest, so beating them is the best sign of a real edge.
Closing-line value (CLV)
How many points better a bet's line was than the closing line.
Expected value (EV)
Average profit per unit risked, from the calibrated cover chance and the price.
Push
A result landing exactly on the line; the stake is returned. Common on 3 and 7.
QB change
An alert that a team's last game was started by someone other than its regular quarterback, read from the box score. It is not a confirmed starter for the next game, so it blocks a bet until the news is checked.
Brier score
Accuracy of probabilities: 0 is perfect, 0.25 is a coin flip.
Units
Profit counting each bet as 1 unit risked at −110.
