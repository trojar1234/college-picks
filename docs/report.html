Backtest Research · not a betting record

This is research, not a record of bets anyone placed or could have placed: it grades against CollegeFootballData's recorded opening and closing lines at −110, and those numbers weren't necessarily on offer at every book at that price. Walk-forward: each week is predicted using only data available before it, then graded against that week's lines. It approximates the live rules but can't replay everything (weather alerts, or the exact lines available on the day; QB changes are replayed from past box scores), and these same seasons were used to choose the 7-point rule, so treat it as an upper bound. "Miss" = average points off (lower is better).

What each column means
  • Bets ATS vs open: games where the model and the opening spread were 7+ points apart, graded against that opening line (when the model's bets are meant to be placed).
  • Bets ATS vs close: the same rule against the closing line, the sharpest line of the week.
  • Bets O/U: the same for totals.
  • All games ATS vs close: the model's side in every game against the closing line, whatever the gap.
  • Approx. live rules: the opening-line bets that today's cautions would still allow (gap under 10, after week 3, no FCS teams).
  • Range: the 95% range for the true win rate given the number of settled bets (pushes excluded). It reflects luck only, not the extra optimism from choosing the rules on these seasons. The week-grouped range allows for bets in the same week moving together.
  • Small grey numbers after a record are pushes. Opening lines exist mostly from 2023 on.

Ranges: green = even the low end clears the 52.4% needed to profit at −110, amber = can't be told apart from break-even yet, red = below break-even.

The cautions

Flagged bets (7+ vs open) that each caution blocks. A caution helps if its blocked bets did worse than the ones it lets through. QB changes are replayed from past box scores (the starter from each team's previous game vs its regular starter). Weather can't be replayed; the live season records it below as a shadow record. A game can fall under more than one caution.

An honest test of the 7-point rule

Choosing the threshold on the same seasons used to grade it flatters the result. Here each season is bet with whichever threshold (5–10 points) made the most units in the seasons before it, so no season helps pick its own rule.

Drift checks this season

Is the model leaning one way this season? Each week's projection was made before the games were played. A lean is marked when it's more than 2.5 standard errors from zero, too big to be chance.

A probability is well calibrated if things it calls 70% happen about 70% of the time. The Brier score measures probability accuracy: 0 is perfect, 0.25 is a coin flip, lower is better.

Ideas tested earlier and dropped

None of these improved accuracy on seasons the model hadn't seen: weather at kickoff, travel and rest, recency weighting, run/pass matchups, fitting the margin directly, a diminishing-returns margin rating, per-team home field, coaching changes, turnover-based preseason weighting, star-rating-based recruiting/portal data, the expected starting QB as a model input (it stays an alert), separate prior strengths per stat, blending ratings with different prior strengths, a median-based (instead of average-based) points fit, a matchup-type totals correction, explosiveness, standard/passing-down efficiency, line yards and stuff rate, a QB adjustment only for new starters, a prior strength that changes through the season, a Power 4 vs Group of 5 margin adjustment, and a machine-learning (gradient-boosted) correction on top of the model. A separate totals model made totals more accurate but its flagged over/under bets did worse against opening lines, so it's off. Retested or added in Oct 2026 under the stricter rule, and still no: rest days, byes and short weeks; travel distance; time zones; altitude; West Coast teams in early Eastern kickoffs; weighting recent games more within a season (worse at every setting); CollegeFootballData's Elo rating as an input (passed 2017–2024, then cut 2025's bet units in half); squared and interaction terms for totals (totals error down for real, but fewer bet wins on the newest season); and different recency weights for the points fit. Tested Oct 27, 2026, and still no: three ways of toning down the home-field edge (it runs about 1.5 points above the market's, but every version won fewer bets); a points conversion that changes with the week of the season (three versions); and a late-season adjustment. The service-academy correction is now applied inside the backtest too, so the backtest matches the live projections.
