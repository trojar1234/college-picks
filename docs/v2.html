How to read this table
  • Model 2.0 is an ensemble of five new models (Bayesian ratings, a possession model, a matchup model, a personnel model and machine learning) plus the current model, weighted by how accurate each was on past seasons it never saw.
  • Score is the projected score (away–home); Win the favorite's chance; 80% range the home team's final margin, which should land in that range 4 times out of 5 (EMU +3 to +20 means Eastern Michigan winning by 3 to 20). Ranges are wider early in the season and for teams the model knows less about.
  • Each game's detail shows how far apart the five new models are. That spread has not predicted accuracy on past seasons (in 2026 the games where they agreed most had the largest misses), so it is not a confidence rating and changes no probability.
  • Line is the sportsbooks' current number, shown for comparison. Lines are never inputs. No bets are recommended: Model 2.0 is in research mode until it proves itself on games it has not seen.
  • Tap a game for each model's number, the likeliest scores and the chances at the current line.

How the backtest works
  • Every number is a walk-forward forecast: each game predicted only from games before it, and every model refit each season on earlier seasons only.
  • Tune (2017–2022) is where every design choice was made. Validate (2023–2024) checked those choices. Test (2025) was opened once, after the design was fixed. 2026 is research: it shaped too many decisions on the current model to count as unseen.
  • Games between two FBS teams only, and every model is scored on the same games; the opening and closing lines on the subset that has them. Average miss is in points; Brier score measures win chances (lower is better); "80% range hit" should be near 80%. For Model 2.0 these come from the same score simulator the page uses.
  • The opening and closing lines are included as forecasts to beat, not as inputs.
